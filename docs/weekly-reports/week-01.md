## Work done:

This week the team identified the research gap by thoroughly analysing existing
government portals (TrackChild, Khoya-Paya, GHAR) and NGO initiatives (ReUnite).
The review revealed that all current solutions are reactive, server-dependent,
and fail during network congestion — precisely when the "golden hour" is most
critical. NCRB data showing 98,375 missing children in 2024 and the Supreme
Court's observation on the urgency of rapid recovery validated the problem.
The team confirmed that no existing system combines instant decentralised
alerting, on-device face recognition, and offline mesh capabilities,
establishing the need for NextGen Rakshak.

In parallel, the team gathered technical grounding: research papers on
MobileFaceNet with TensorFlow Lite for edge deployment, Google ML Kit
documentation for face detection, and the Nearby Connections API for
peer-to-peer communication. This foundational research directly targets the
"golden hour" problem by defining a system architecture that operates
independently of unreliable internet infrastructure during mass gatherings.
